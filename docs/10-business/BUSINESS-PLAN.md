# Kimosabe Commons — Public Benefit Growth and Stewardship Company

> **Working name:** The Commons Plan  
> **Status:** A proposal for founder decision. Kimosabe Commons, PBC does not yet exist. Nothing in this plan binds Donald Haight, Market Applications LLC, United Stakeholders of America, LLC, RRCA, or a future entity.

## Executive Summary

Kimosabe Commons, PBC is a proposed Delaware public benefit corporation for the recruiting, promotion, stewardship, and verification layer of the Kimosabe App and Human Blockchain. The immediate problem is practical: the founder presently connects concepts, territories, contractors, Sponsors, civic partners, builders, and evidence. That dependence is difficult to repeat, measure, or hand off. The proposed company turns that work into four programs: Recruiting and Roster Operations; Promotion and Campaign Production; Territory Stewardship; and Attestation and Verification.

Its public-benefit purpose is measurable rather than decorative: verified, address-anchored participants, evidenced completions, and retained participation by territory and Season. The company would begin with a bounded, one-Season pilot, not national deployment. It is not governance software sold to DAO operators. Its differentiation is recruiting and stewardship of a verified map of People and Addresses: finding participants, recording consent and role eligibility, assigning territory, running a campaign, preserving evidence, and closing a Season fairly.

This plan uses a transparent founder model, not external market statistics. Its illustrative nationwide seat model is 3,140 planning territories × 15 serviceable roster seats × a $1,200 Season price = $56.52 million. That is a model output, not a market fact or revenue promise. The base case is twelve pilot territories in Year 1, forty-five in Year 2, and 120 in Year 3. Required controls include documented IP rights, separate entity records, clear Sponsor disclosures, counsel review before regulated activity, a benefit report distinguishing reported activity from verified evidence, and transparent, timely correction of any identified material reporting errors.

## 1. The Problem

### 1.1 The founder-as-middleware bottleneck

**Founder intention.** The Human Blockchain treats People and Addresses as the two primitives; a documented **Need** can become an evidenced **Done** through roles, territories, and a seasonal cycle. It does not recruit, explain, verify, schedule, or follow up by itself. Today, the founder is the connection point among the idea, operating entities, potential users, territories, and evidence.

That is a normal early-stage bottleneck, not a criticism. A contractor cannot reliably see an available role; a Sponsor cannot see the evidence delivered; a Recruiter lacks a common roster workflow; and a builder cannot infer consent, authority, or data handling from a role title. Progress therefore relies on memory and bespoke follow-up, making acquisition cost, retention, and accountability hard to compare. Kimosabe Commons would turn those links into routine records and operating work.

### 1.2 Fragmented recruiting and weak attribution

Contractors and Crews, Sponsors and Institutions, Civic and County Partners, and Engineers and Agent Builders arrive for different reasons and through different channels. Without a common funnel, introductions become isolated conversations rather than a roster.

The company would use a consent-based path from Prospect through Lead, qualification, verification where applicable, orientation, role assignment, activation, retention, reactivation, or exit. The objective is not contact volume; it is a roster with known role, territory, authority, term, evidence requirement, and communications permission. This fills the documented gap: the evaluated DAO stacks do not provide a recruiting CRM from outreach and consent through placement, retention, and offboarding. [SRC — research/00-dao-platform-landscape.md]

The company would also connect a consented campaign or referral source to qualified recruit, activation, evidence, and retention. It would report approved aggregates, not applicant histories, sensitive records, or personal addresses.

### 1.3 Unverifiable participation and the seasonal reset

A role label, wallet, email, or profile does not establish county relationship, consent, professional status, availability, or work quality. The reviewed platforms begin from wallet, account, or governance membership; Decidim has useful participation tiers, but its release-specific verification requires validation. None supplies the required county eligibility, territory, and applicant workflow. [SRC — research/00-dao-platform-landscape.md; SRC — research/06-06-public-benefit-adjacent.md]

The company would attest only to its defined process: that requested evidence was provided and reviewed, a role was assigned for a Season, or task evidence was attached. It would not certify licensure, insurance coverage, legal compliance, workmanship, underwriting, or claims outcomes.

At the boundary, every Address returns to **Need**. Roles, seats, and baselines renew while history remains. The reset corrects stale records and makes retention measurable: a participant must be active again, not merely remain counted.

### 1.4 The missing layer in the DAO tooling market

No evaluated platform supplies recruiting CRM, campaign attribution, seasonal credit administration, verified real-world identity and territory, or a full General Ledger. [SRC — research/00-dao-platform-landscape.md] Aragon, DAOhaus, Colony, DAOstack, and Snapshot/Tally focus on permissions, wallets, proposals, voting, execution, or contribution records; Open Collective, Loomio, and Decidim offer useful financial, deliberative, and participation patterns. None operates a county roster.

The opportunity is therefore not voting software. It is the missing operating layer: recruiting, verifying, promoting, assigning, evidencing, disputing, retaining, and reporting. Governance technology can later be a bounded adapter, not the product thesis.

## 2. The Proposition and the Chartered Benefit

### 2.1 Plain-language proposition

Kimosabe Commons would help a territory form and maintain a usable roster: identify who is needed, invite them through a documented funnel, review the evidence for a scoped role, assign that role for a Season, run local promotion, and retain a record of completion and open work.

Its working thesis is: **Every address is a stakeholder. Every stakeholder gets a job.** A person can hold a defined, time-bounded Designation—such as Recruiter, Campaign Producer, Verifier, Territory Steward, Contractor, or Captain—rather than merely be counted as a follower. A **Group** is a structural category with its own management system, Primary Admin, ledger accounts, and rules; a **Designation** is a functional role within it. The Commons Engine is the proposed internal software name. It supports the institution; it does not replace clear mandates or human review.

### 2.2 Chartered public benefit

The following wording is proposed for the charter and is reproduced verbatim:

> Increasing the number of verified, address-anchored, lawfully organized participants who can convert a documented community Need into a documented, evidenced Done — measured as verified stakeholder activations, evidenced completions, and retained participation per territory per season.

The benefit is tested through verified participation, documented Need-to-Done transitions, and territorial, seasonal retention—not audience size or unreviewed claims. It does not promise service to every address, acceptance of every applicant, or any change in professional, financial, legal, or insurance status.

### 2.3 Operating boundaries

Kimosabe Commons would market, recruit, promote, manage community operations, and administer defined governance processes. It would create ledger candidates and reconciliation queues, not move money or replace accounting. Consequential records must identify actor, entity, Group, role, authority, object, evidence, ledger effect, and review/dispute path.

It is not an insurer, adjuster, escrow agent, money transmitter, bank, law firm, fiduciary, or token issuer. Roster operations cannot imply a participant is licensed, insured, compliant, or endorsed.

Legal, privacy, or operational constraints that make a seat too variable also invalidate it. The pilot exists to replace assumptions with measured internal evidence.

### 4.2 Realistic non-crypto substitutes

The more immediate alternatives are not DAO platforms. They are organizations that already purchase attention, recruitment, local activation, or member management.

| Substitute category | Overlap | Differentiation and constraint |
|---|---|---|

### 4.3 Strategic stance

Lead with the market white space, not crypto framing: “We recruit and verify the roster for your territory, run the campaign, preserve evidence, and close the Season.” Any governance adapter remains optional and subordinate.

## 5. Product and Operating Model

### 5.1 The four programs

| Program | What the company does | Deliverable / evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Recruiting and Roster Operations** | Sources, consents, reviews, assigns, renews, and exits candidates | Roster, source, role/term, status, exception record | No licensing or insurance determination. |
| **2. Promotion and Campaign Production** | Produces campaign briefs, content, invitations, event/direct-mail plans, and announcements | Approved plan, versions, channel/source log, learning record | No promise of leads, revenue, or media result. |
| **3. Territory Stewardship** | Allocates seats; handles renewal, dispute, reset, and register changes | Territory register, decision, close, and appeal record | Time-bounded designation, not property or permanence. |
| **4. Attestation and Verification** | Reviews defined process evidence and issues scoped attestations | Reviewer, scope, date, evidence, limitation, dispute record | Process attestation only; no certification. |

### 5.2 Seasonal operating calendar

The five phases below provide the operating and evidence calendar.

| Phase | Window | Company work | Required control / output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-Season** | January–February | Select territories, recruit, publish rules, form teams, train | Plan, disclosures, consents, roles, budget, readiness decision |
| **Season** | March–November | Run campaigns, activate roster, collect evidence, attest, check retention | Dashboard, evidence/exception queues, reconciliation candidates |
| **Cool-Down** | December 1 | Stop new work, collect receivables through responsible entity, calculate provisional records | Close checklist, receivable status, preliminary benefit metrics |
| **Dispute Resolution** | December–February | Resolve timely role, attribution, evidence, and record challenges | Dispute, recusal, rationale, appeal, close records |
| **Reset** | Season boundary | Return every Address to Need; renew seats/baselines; preserve history | Reset certificate and new-Season baseline |

The calendar does not bypass agreement, professional requirements, entity approval, or ledger control.

### 5.3 Funnel and state model

The operating funnel is **discover → consent → Prospect → Lead → qualification → verification → orientation → role/territory → activation → evidence → retention, reactivation, or exit**. Verification is dated, scoped, owned, evidenced, and renewable. The work funnel is **Prospect → Lead → Offer → Pending Project → Project → Job → Job Order → Task Request → Task → evidence → Done**. “Due Today” means acknowledge, act, document a dependency, or set the next step—not physical completion.

## 6. Revenue Model and Unit Economics

### 6.1 Disclosed revenue streams

The proposed revenue model has four streams. All amounts and margins in this section are **model output**, not historical results, market facts, or guarantees.

| Stream | Illustrative unit and price | Direct cost per unit | Gross margin | What is purchased |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Sponsor and founding-seat programs | $7,500 per territory per Season | $2,100 | 72.0% | Defined research, implementation, measurement, and recognition package; no ownership, profit participation, performance-linked payment, or financial-upside right |
| Territory and seat subscription | $1,200 per roster seat per Season | $400 | 66.7% | Seat administration, roster support, renewal and a defined service level |
| Campaign and content production services | $4,000 per territory campaign package | $1,700 | 57.5% | Approved campaign brief, production, local activation support, and reporting scope |
| Attestation and verification services | $125 per completed seat/process check; $1,000 illustrative eight-seat territory package | $400 per package | 60.0% | Defined process attestation and evidence review, with stated limitations |

The sponsor program must be described as a purchase of a defined package. It must not be represented as a security, a fractional ownership interest, a claim on company performance, or a right to control operations. A sponsor disclosure should state the deliverables, recognition terms, cancellation terms, data limits, and what is expressly not being sold.

### 6.2 Per-seat assumptions

| Per-seat assumption | Model output | Formula / note |
|---|---:|---|
| Season seat price | $1,200 | Illustrative territory-and-seat subscription price |
| Cost to recruit a qualified seat | $175 | Sourcing, outreach, screening coordination, orientation allocation |
| Cost to verify a roster seat | $100 | Evidence review, exception handling, record preparation; not professional certification |
| Seat support and platform allocation | $125 | Stewardship, communications, software/tools, renewal administration |
| Total direct cost per seat | $400 | $175 + $100 + $125 |
| Gross contribution per paid seat | $800 | $1,200 − $400 |
| Seat-program gross margin | 66.7% | $800 ÷ $1,200 |

The recruiting-and-verification component is $275 per seat in the base model. A seat is not “verified” merely because a fee is collected. The cost assumes a defined, repeatable internal check, a documented exception path, and no claim that the company has replaced the participant’s legal or professional obligations.

### 6.3 One illustrative county Season

**Model output.** The base county assumes eight paid seats and one purchase of each other service package. It is a planning unit, not a required bundle or a promise that a county produces this mix.

| County Season line item | Revenue | Direct cost | Gross contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Eight territory/seat subscriptions | $9,600 | $3,200 | $6,400 |
| Sponsor/founding-seat program | $7,500 | $2,100 | $5,400 |
| Campaign and content production | $4,000 | $1,700 | $2,300 |
| Attestation and verification package | $1,000 | $400 | $600 |
| **County Season total** | **$22,100** | **$7,400** | **$14,700** |

The **model output cost of running one Season in one county is $13,000**, composed of $7,400 direct cost plus $5,600 of allocated local operating cost: Territory Lead allocation $1,800; quality/dispute review $1,100; local outreach or event support $1,400; tools, records, and local administration $1,300. This produces a county-level operating contribution of $9,100 before central pilot setup costs. The allocation must be replaced with actual time and vendor records during the pilot.

### 6.4 Payback and break-even

**Model output.** The illustrative recruitment-and-verification spend is $275 per paid seat. Its payback occurs at the point the $1,200 seat charge is collected, because direct seat contribution is $800 after all $400 direct costs. If the seat charge is collected in installments, payback is delayed until cumulative collected margin exceeds $275; the model does not assume a payment schedule.

The base case assigns **$72,000** of central pilot setup cost to Season 1: operating manual, privacy/consent documentation, basic software configuration, founder/administrative coordination, training, quality assurance, and launch materials. At $14,700 gross contribution per county bundle, break-even is:

```text
Central Season setup cost ÷ county gross contribution
$72,000 ÷ $14,700 = 4.90 counties
Base-model break-even: 5 completed county bundles
```

The following sensitivity holds sponsor, campaign, and attestation package contribution at $8,300 per county and varies only seat price and combined recruit/verify cost. Seat-support allocation remains $125 per seat. Values are **model output** and show the rounded number of county bundles required to cover $72,000 central setup cost.

| Seat price / recruit + verify cost | $200 | $275 | $350 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $800 seat price | 6 counties | 7 counties | 7 counties |
| $1,200 seat price | 5 counties | 5 counties | 6 counties |
| $1,600 seat price | 4 counties | 5 counties | 5 counties |

The sensitivity does not validate the prices or costs. It shows why the company must learn early whether qualified recruiting can be conducted at a repeatable cost and whether the full service bundle is understandable enough to purchase.

### 6.5 Revenue controls

Revenue should be recognized only in the records of the entity that has the written customer agreement and should be reconciled to the external General Ledger. The Commons Engine may create invoice, service-delivery, or reconciliation candidates; it must not be presented as accounting software or a money-moving system. Material disbursements require dual authorization and a separation between local campaign operation and finance/compliance review, following the fiscal-host separation pattern researched in Open Collective. [SRC — research/06-06-public-benefit-adjacent.md]

## 8. Entity, Ownership and Capital Strategy

### 8.1 Proposed entity architecture

**Kimosabe Commons, PBC** is proposed as a Delaware public benefit corporation. Delaware is the proposed jurisdiction because the intended form is a public benefit corporation with a stated, measurable public benefit and a benefit-reporting discipline. This is a proposed legal choice, not legal advice or a statement that incorporation is complete. Counsel must confirm formation, charter terms, governance duties, qualification requirements, tax treatment, employment classification, privacy obligations, and any state-by-state implications.

The proposed company would sit beside—not silently inside—existing entities.

- **Market Applications LLC** exists today as the technology / Genesis entity.
- **United Stakeholders of America, LLC (USA LLC)** exists today as the founder-controlled beta container, a Florida LLC with an S-corporation election and assumed names described in the engagement brief.
- A future **United Stakeholders of America foundation** is a future-state concept only. The intended analogy to a foundation role is directional, not a formed organization, approval, partnership, or legal commitment.
- **RRCA** is independently owned and is Customer Zero: an operating laboratory and source of workflow evidence. Its revenue, licenses, assets, liabilities, goodwill, and operating decisions remain outside the platform.

### 8.2 IP, ownership, and conflicts posture

The founder currently retains domains, brands, and scheduled founder IP. Kimosabe Commons may receive a documented license, assignment, option, or other written right only when executed by the relevant parties. It does not silently absorb founder IP, Market Applications technology, USA LLC assets, RRCA data, or another entity’s goodwill because the names or mission overlap. A DBA does not create a separate entity, ownership boundary, tax identity, liability shield, or enterprise asset.

The conflict-of-interest posture should be written before the pilot: disclose the founder’s overlapping authority; identify transactions involving founder-controlled entities; require a written scope, pricing rationale, and approval record for related-party work; preserve a recusal record where a later board or independent reviewer is responsible; and distinguish Customer Zero evidence from a generalizable customer claim. At launch, the founder is the sole decision authority according to the engagement brief. That concentration must be reported, not disguised by committee language.

### 8.3 Capital and sponsorship separation

The company should fund early work through earned service revenue, founder-authorized operating support, carefully documented Sponsor packages, and other lawful pathways selected after counsel review. A Sponsor purchase is a defined research, implementation, measurement, and recognition package. It is not a security, a financial participation product, an ownership grant, or an entitlement to a return.

No capital pathway should be described publicly as a securities transaction, and no ownership-related instrument, right, or solicitation should be offered or discussed as available without securities counsel. Any future pathway involving outside capital, ownership interests, profit participation, convertible rights, or similar instruments requires securities counsel before design, communication, or acceptance. The company will not use sponsorship language as a substitute label for an unreviewed securities transaction.

## 9. Measurement and Public-Benefit Reporting

### 9.1 Governing metrics

The proposed benefit report should use a small metric set that follows the chartered benefit and separates activity from outcome.

| Metric | Definition | Evidence class | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verified stakeholder activations | Participants with a dated role, territory, Season, completed required process checks, and active status | **Verified** only when an internal reviewer confirms the required record | Monthly; Season close |
| Evidenced completions | Need-to-Done transitions with linked task evidence and an attestation scope | **Verified** only for completed review; otherwise **reported** | Monthly; Season close |
| Retained participation | Prior-Season active participants who complete new-Season renewal and activation | **Verified** against successive Season rosters | Season boundary |
| Time to qualified activation | Median elapsed days from consented Lead to active role | **Reported** from operating records; methodology published | Monthly |
| Cost to recruit and verify a seat | Direct, attributable recruiting and verification cost divided by completed qualified seats | **Reported** from reconciled internal records; independently checked only if an external review occurs | Monthly; Season close |
| Dispute and correction rate | Timely disputes or corrections divided by relevant role, evidence, or territory records | **Reported**, with category and disposition | Monthly; Season close |
| Data and process incidents | Confirmed privacy, authorization, or evidence-control incidents, categorized by severity | **Verified** by incident owner after internal review | Prompt notice where material; quarterly summary |

### 9.2 Cadence, publication, and challenge

During a pilot, the company should issue an internal monthly operations record and an external Season report after close and dispute resolution. The report should publish definitions, numerator, denominator, territory scope, measurement window, methodology changes, known limitations, and whether each figure is **reported**, **verified by defined internal evidence**, or **not independently assured**. It should not publish personal data, home addresses, applicant dossiers, or confidential commercial terms.

A reader should be able to challenge a figure through a published email or form, with the metric name, Season, territory, claimed error, and supporting evidence. The company should acknowledge the challenge, assign an owner, record the decision, correct the report where warranted, and publish a dated correction note. A challenge mechanism is not an assurance that every claim will be accepted; it is a requirement that a challenge cannot disappear into an informal conversation.

## 13. How to Read This Document

This plan separates three evidence classes so a reader can challenge the right thing.

| Label | Meaning in this plan | How it should be read |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder intention** | A direction, operating preference, or proposed decision supplied through the engagement brief | It is authoritative for the proposal’s intended shape, but it is not proof of legal availability, customer demand, or completed execution |
| **Model output** | Arithmetic derived from stated assumptions, including market sizing, unit economics, break-even, and scenarios | It is only as sound as its inputs. It is not a verified market fact, historical result, forecast, or guarantee |
| **Verified fact** | A fact supported in the supplied research by an observed source, or an internal record that meets the stated evidence standard | It remains limited to its source, date, and scope; it does not prove an unsourced conclusion |
| **REPORTED** | A statement supplied by a founder, vendor, customer, or other source without independent confirmation in this plan | It may guide a test, but it is not upgraded into fact |
| **INFERRED** | A reasoned conclusion drawn from the evidence available | It should be tested and may be wrong |
| **ASSUMED** | A planning premise used because no verified fact is available | It is a candidate for immediate validation or removal |

Research citations use the form `[SRC — research/file-name.md]` and identify the supplied evidence file, not an implied endorsement by any named platform. No number should be read as verified market data unless this document expressly says it is a verified fact. Nothing in this plan is legal, accounting, insurance, tax, or securities advice.

## 14. Open Questions — Founder Decision Required

The following questions can only be answered by the founder or through a founder-authorized decision. They are deliberately not resolved by this plan.

1. **Name decision:** Is Kimosabe Commons, PBC the approved legal and operating name, or should one of the retained alternatives—United Stakeholders Growth, PBC; The Stakeholder Guild, PBC; Addressable Commons, PBC; or Human Blockchain Growth Foundation—advance for legal review?
2. **Initial buyer:** Who is the first paying buyer for a Season seat or package: a Contractor, a local operating Group, a Sponsor, a civic-facing organization, or an entity controlled by the founder? What exact problem will that buyer acknowledge in writing?
3. **Seat definition:** Which roles are eligible for the first eight seats in a territory, which are paid, and which are volunteer, staff, or partner roles? What evidence is required for each?
4. **Territory rule:** What does a territory allocation allow, exclude, and obligate? What makes it renewable, reviewable, or revocable before the reset?
5. **Season rulebook:** Which decisions are advisory, delegated, administrative, or binding in Season 1? Who can challenge each decision, and who gives the final internal answer?
6. **Founder IP boundary:** Which domains, brands, software, workflows, content, data structures, and relationships are licensed to the proposed PBC, on what term, for what consideration, and with what termination rights?
7. **RRCA relationship:** What precise Customer Zero scope, data boundary, service fee if any, attribution permission, and public reference permission will RRCA accept in writing?
8. **Sponsor package:** What is the exact first sponsor package, its price, deliverables, recognition language, data access, cancellation treatment, and disclosure approved by counsel?
9. **Capital policy:** What early operating support is founder-authorized, which expenses can be incurred before formation, and which future capital pathways should be explored only after counsel review?
10. **Public-benefit threshold:** What minimum verified activations, evidenced completions, retention, and correction performance would justify expanding beyond a pilot territory?
11. **Governance transition:** When, and under what trigger, will the founder add independent board or advisory capacity, conflict review, and delegated operating authority?
12. **Stop condition:** What result would cause the founder to end, pause, or materially narrow the first Season rather than continue on narrative momentum?
